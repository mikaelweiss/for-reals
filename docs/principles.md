# Principles

Each principle answers a problem in [problems.md](problems.md).

1. **Real life is the tiebreaker.** When unsure how something should behave, copy nature.
2. **The world changes on its own.** Plants grow and die, herds move, seasons turn, with no player involved.
3. **Everything depends on something.** Weather drives plants. Plants feed prey. Prey feed predators. No system stands alone.
4. **Patterns, not dice.** Weather and animals follow causes you can learn. Randomness adds variety but never decides the outcome.
5. **Signs before consequences.** Anything that can hurt you shows a warning in the world first: clouds building, wind shifting, animals fleeing. Never in a menu.
6. **Ignoring the world costs you. Paying attention is always enough.** Real stakes, no unfair deaths.
7. **One set of rules.** Farms, pens, and players obey the same rules as the wild. No special cases.
8. **Balance comes from the rules, not caps.** Food and predators hold populations in check. Spawn caps are a safety net, never the mechanism.
9. **Nothing is infinite, nothing is gone forever.** You can hunt an area empty. It refills slowly from its neighbors.
10. **The world lives where nobody is looking.** Areas with no player nearby keep going, simulated cheaply.
11. **Core holds every rule.** Extras adds looks and content only. A rule never requires a client mod.
12. **It runs well on a family server.** A feature that lags a 4 GB server is not done.

## Rulings

- **Existing worlds work.** Seasons, weather, and animals apply everywhere. New terrain appears only in chunks generated after install.
- **An empty server pauses.** The calendar and every simulation stop when no player is online.
- **Sleep passes time, it does not skip it.** Crops grow, animals eat, and weather continues through the night. Sleeping never clears a storm.
- **Difficulty sets harshness.** The vanilla difficulty setting scales how much climate and predators can hurt a player.
